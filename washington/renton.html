Renton, Washington

Renton is one of those Puget Sound cities that reveals itself gradually. At first glance, it can seem like part of the continuous urban fabric stretching southeast from Seattle, but spend a little time here and the city’s own geography starts to assert itself. The Cedar River meets Lake Washington at Renton’s edge, wooded hills rise behind neighborhoods, and the downtown streets carry traces of an older industrial town beneath the newer restaurants, apartments, offices and public spaces.

The story of Renton begins long before the modern city. The area was part of the homeland of the Duwamish people, whose relationship with the rivers, forests and wetlands predates settlement by thousands of years. In the nineteenth century, settlers arrived and the discovery and development of coal transformed the area into a mining and manufacturing community. Erasmus Smithers platted the town in 1875, naming it for Captain William Renton, and the city incorporated in 1901.

Then Renton changed again. Pacific Car and Foundry, later PACCAR, became an important employer, while Boeing’s arrival during World War II transformed the city into a major center of aircraft manufacturing. Boeing’s Renton plant later became closely associated with the production of commercial jets, giving the city a place in aviation history that remains visible today. At the same time, waves of immigrants and new residents have continually reshaped Renton’s neighborhoods, businesses and cultural life.
