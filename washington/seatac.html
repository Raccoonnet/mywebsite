SeaTac, Washington

SeaTac is a city built around movement. Aircraft rise over neighborhoods, light-rail trains carry passengers north toward Seattle and south through the region, and Interstate 5 keeps traffic flowing between Puget Sound’s major cities. Yet beneath all that motion is a surprisingly layered community: a place of lakes and parks, immigrant-owned businesses, gardens, longtime neighborhoods and a history that began decades before an airport ever appeared on the map.

The city incorporated in 1990, making SeaTac relatively young as an incorporated municipality, but the story of the land is considerably older. Duwamish and Muckleshoot people used the area for generations, traveling between inland areas and the saltwater shorelines. Later came Military Road, farms and small settlements, followed by dramatic growth during World War II as defense industries expanded. The airport began developing in the area in 1942, and its presence ultimately became so defining that the new city took its name from the familiar abbreviation for Seattle-Tacoma International Airport.

Today, SeaTac is home to roughly 32,000 residents, but its daytime population can swell enormously because of the airport, hotels, workers and travelers moving through the city. That constant exchange gives SeaTac an international character unusual for a city of its size. More than anything, the city feels like a crossroads: a place where people arrive, depart, work, settle down and build communities of their own.
