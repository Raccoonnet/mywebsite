Oak Harbor, Washington

Oak Harbor sits on the northern end of Whidbey Island, where the shoreline curves around a sheltered harbor and the land opens toward forests, farms, beaches and long views across Puget Sound. On clear days, the Cascade Mountains provide a dramatic backdrop across the water, while the Olympic Mountains appear in the opposite direction. The city feels distinctly maritime, but it is not simply a waterfront town. Oak Harbor is also a military community, an agricultural community, a family town and the largest city on Whidbey Island, with its own rhythm shaped by residents who have put down roots here.

Long before today’s streets and marinas, Southern Coast Salish peoples lived around these waters, relying on the harbor and surrounding shoreline for fishing, shellfish, travel and trade. Euro-American settlement followed in the mid-1800s, including Irish settlers and sea captains who helped establish the early commercial center. Dutch families arrived later in the nineteenth century, leaving another enduring thread in Oak Harbor’s agricultural and cultural history. The city’s name itself reflects the Garry oak trees that once characterized the surrounding prairies.

Then came the Navy. Naval Air Station Whidbey Island was commissioned in 1942, transforming what had been a small island community and establishing a military presence that remains central to Oak Harbor’s identity and economy. Today, that history exists alongside historic downtown buildings, Windjammer Park, the marina, waterfront trails and a community that continues to evolve. The result is a place where military aircraft can cross the sky while sailboats move through the harbor below—a particularly Oak Harbor combination.
