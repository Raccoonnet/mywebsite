Burlington, Washington

Burlington is the kind of Pacific Northwest town that makes more sense once you slow down. The Skagit River traces the city’s southern and southeastern edges, broad agricultural fields stretch beyond the neighborhoods, and the Cascade Mountains rise in the distance. On a clear morning, the landscape has an almost theatrical contrast: green fields below, dark evergreen hills beyond them, and a huge Northwest sky overhead. Then the clouds roll in, the colors deepen, and Burlington settles into the softer, wetter atmosphere that is equally characteristic of this corner of Washington.

The city’s story has always been tied to movement. Burlington grew from a logging and agricultural community into a railroad center, earning its longtime nickname, the Hub City, because rail lines once radiated from the town like spokes. William McKay filed the town plat in 1891, and Burlington incorporated in 1902. Today, the railroad remains part of the landscape, while Interstate 5 and State Route 20 make the city a natural crossroads for travelers moving through Skagit County.

That crossroads location is part of what makes Burlington interesting. It is not trying to be a resort town or a preserved historical village. It is a working community with farms nearby, a busy retail and service economy, parks, restaurants, cafés, community events, and easy access to the larger landscape of the Skagit Valley. Spend some time here and you begin to see how the city functions as a practical home base for exploring northwestern Washington while still having a character of its own.
