Lynnwood, Washington

Lynnwood is the kind of place where the geography of the Pacific Northwest becomes part of everyday life. It sits between Seattle and Everett, at the meeting point of Interstate 5 and Interstate 405, close enough to the urban energy of the Puget Sound region while retaining its own distinctly suburban rhythm. On a clear morning, the surrounding landscape opens toward mountains and forests; on a rainy afternoon, the streets take on that familiar Northwest sheen, with coffee shops, restaurants, and neighborhood businesses carrying on beneath gray skies.

What looks today like a busy crossroads has a much older story underneath it. Before highways and shopping centers, this was a remote part of south Snohomish County where reaching Seattle by horse-drawn wagon could take two days. The area later became Alderwood Manor, a planned agricultural community created after extensive logging, with a demonstration farm intended to teach prospective settlers how to raise chickens and make productive use of logged-over land. An interurban railway connected the area with Seattle and Everett, and for a time Alderwood became one of the country’s significant egg-producing communities.

The automobile eventually reshaped everything. Highway 99 arrived in 1927, the Interurban stopped running in 1939, Interstate 5 followed in the 1960s, and Alderwood Mall helped establish Lynnwood as a regional shopping destination. The city incorporated in 1959 with about 6,000 residents and embraced the nickname “Hub City.” Today, Lynnwood remains a place defined by movement and connection, but the meaning of that connection is changing again: Link light rail now reaches Lynnwood City Center, giving the city a new relationship with Seattle and the broader regional transit network.
