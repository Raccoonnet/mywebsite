Eatonville, Washington

Eatonville sits where the roads of rural Pierce County begin to feel distinctly like mountain roads. Forest rises around the town, the Little Mashel River cuts through the landscape, and on a clear day Mount Rainier—Tahoma to the Indigenous peoples of the region—appears beyond the trees. It is an easy place to pass through on the way toward Mount Rainier National Park, but Eatonville rewards anyone who slows down long enough to notice its older buildings, independent businesses, parks, and the rhythm of a community that has spent more than a century living alongside the forest.

The town’s story reaches back much further than its incorporation in 1909. The area was home to Nisqually people, and Nisqually guide Indian Henry played an important role in the history of Eatonville and the routes toward Mount Rainier. Thomas C. Van Eaton arrived here in the late 1880s, established a trading post, and helped turn the settlement into a waypoint for people traveling toward the mountain. The Tacoma Eastern Railroad arrived in the early 1900s, followed by the lumber industry, and Eatonville grew around timber, transportation, farming, and the steady movement of people between Puget Sound and the mountain.

Today, Eatonville has a population of roughly 2,900 and retains the feeling of a working small town rather than a resort community. Downtown is compact enough to explore on foot, while the surrounding countryside opens quickly into forests, lakes, trails, farms, and wildlife habitat. That combination is what makes Eatonville interesting: it is not simply a place to stop before an adventure elsewhere. It is a community with its own history, food, gathering places, and relationship with the landscape.
