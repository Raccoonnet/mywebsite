West Fargo, North Dakota

West Fargo sits just west of the Red River and across the river valley from Minnesota, part of the rapidly expanding Fargo-Moorhead metropolitan area. What was once a small agricultural community has grown into one of North Dakota’s largest cities, with new neighborhoods, businesses, schools, parks, and gathering places continually changing the landscape. Yet even as development spreads across former farmland, West Fargo still carries traces of the prairie community it grew from.

The city’s history began with the railroad and agriculture. Established in the late 19th century as a railroad town, West Fargo became a service and shipping point for farmers working the fertile Red River Valley. That agricultural connection remains important to the area’s identity, even as modern West Fargo has developed a much broader economy and increasingly functions as a residential and commercial center within the Fargo-Moorhead region.

Spend a little time here and the city’s character becomes easier to understand. There are quiet residential streets, busy commercial corridors, recreational trails, community parks, youth sports, locally owned restaurants, and an energetic population that reflects the area’s growth. West Fargo may be young compared with many North Dakota communities, but it is already developing a personality of its own—one that balances suburban convenience with the open landscape and neighborly sensibility of the Great Plains.
