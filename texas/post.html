Post, Texas

There is a particular feeling to arriving in Post, Texas. The road runs across the broad West Texas landscape, the horizon seems unusually open, and then the town appears along the edge of the Llano Estacado, where the high plains meet the dramatic drop of the Caprock Escarpment. Post is about 40 miles southeast of Lubbock and sits at the crossroads of U.S. Highways 84 and 380, making it an easy place to pass through—but its history gives travelers plenty of reasons to stop.

Unlike many West Texas towns, Post was deliberately planned from the beginning. C. W. Post, the cereal entrepreneur behind Post Toasties and Postum, purchased roughly 200,000 acres of ranchland and established the town in 1907 as an ambitious model community. Streets were lined with trees, homes and civic buildings were carefully planned, and the Double U Company developed businesses, farms and infrastructure around the new settlement. The railroad arrived in 1910, helping transform Post from an experiment on the plains into a functioning regional town.

Today, Post remains the county seat of Garza County and a center for the surrounding ranching and agricultural country. Its population has changed over the decades, rising with the textile and oil industries before settling into a smaller modern community. What remains especially appealing is the combination of old and open: historic downtown buildings, ranch-country traditions, wide skies, local museums and the unmistakable presence of the Caprock just beyond town.
