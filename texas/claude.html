Claude, Texas

Claude is the kind of Texas Panhandle town where the horizon does as much storytelling as the buildings do. Located about 30 miles east of Amarillo, Claude sits in north-central Armstrong County on the edge of the Llano Estacado, with the Caprock Escarpment and the canyon country of the Texas Panhandle nearby. The landscape is broad, dry and intensely open, but south of town the land suddenly begins to fold into ravines and breaks, creating the striking scenery around Tule Canyon and the upper reaches of the Palo Duro country.

Claude itself began with the railroad. When the Fort Worth and Denver City Railway reached the area in 1887, the settlement was initially called Armstrong City, but it was soon renamed Claude after Claude Ayers, the railroad engineer who brought the first train through. When Armstrong County was organized in 1890, Claude won the competition to become the county seat, reportedly with legendary cattleman Charles Goodnight providing the deciding vote. That combination of railroad, ranching and county government gave the little town a lasting role in the Panhandle.

There is also a cinematic side to Claude. The surrounding landscape and the town itself have appeared in several films, most famously Hud, the 1963 Paul Newman film, as well as Leap of Faith and Sunshine Christmas. Even without a movie camera nearby, Claude has the kind of setting that makes you understand why filmmakers noticed it: weathered storefronts, old courthouse architecture, grain elevators, ranch country and enormous sunsets stretching across the plains.
