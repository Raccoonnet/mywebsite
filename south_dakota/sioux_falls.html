Sioux Falls, South Dakota

Sioux Falls is a city that reveals itself gradually. Start downtown and you can hear traffic, conversation, and the clatter of a busy coffee shop; walk a few minutes toward Falls Park and the sound changes to rushing water as the Big Sioux River tumbles over layers of pinkish quartzite. That contrast is part of the city’s character. Sioux Falls feels unmistakably Midwestern—practical, friendly, and unhurried—yet its downtown, neighborhoods, restaurants, galleries, and growing creative scene give it an energy that goes well beyond its size.

The river has always been central to the story. Long before Sioux Falls became South Dakota’s largest city, the falls served as a gathering place for Indigenous peoples, including the Dakota, and later attracted traders, settlers, and entrepreneurs. The city’s growth followed the river, railroads, agriculture, and eventually a diversified economy, but the landscape remains surprisingly present in everyday life. On a summer evening, families gather near the falls, cyclists follow the river, and residents stroll through downtown as if the water has always been the city’s natural meeting point—which, in many ways, it has.

There is also something pleasantly approachable about Sioux Falls. You can spend a morning exploring its historic core, eat lunch made with ingredients from the region, wander through a sculpture garden, and still have time to watch the light change over the river before dinner. It is a place where the best experiences often come from slowing down enough to notice what locals already know is there.
