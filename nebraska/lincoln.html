Lincoln, Nebraska

Lincoln has a way of making visitors feel welcome from the moment they arrive. As Nebraska’s capital city, it combines the energy of a thriving university, the charm of historic neighborhoods, and the openness of the Great Plains into a destination that feels both vibrant and approachable. Wide streets lined with mature trees lead to bustling districts filled with local restaurants, bookstores, galleries, and cafés, while expansive parks and scenic trails remind you that nature is never far away. Whether you’re visiting for a Cornhuskers game, a weekend getaway, or a cross-country road trip, Lincoln offers a refreshing balance of city life and Midwestern hospitality.

Founded in 1867 when Nebraska achieved statehood, Lincoln was purposefully chosen as the new capital and named in honor of President Abraham Lincoln. Over the decades, it has grown into one of the Midwest’s leading centers for education, government, healthcare, and innovation. The University of Nebraska–Lincoln has helped shape the city’s identity, bringing together students, researchers, artists, and entrepreneurs from around the world. Yet despite its growth, Lincoln has retained a relaxed pace that encourages visitors to explore its neighborhoods, meet its people, and enjoy the simple pleasures of everyday life.

Spend a few days in Lincoln, and you’ll quickly notice that the city isn’t defined by a single landmark or attraction. It’s the combination of lively downtown streets, quiet parks, public art, local traditions, and welcoming residents that creates its distinctive character. Every season brings something new, from colorful autumn football weekends to lively summer festivals and peaceful winter evenings beneath Nebraska’s wide-open skies.
