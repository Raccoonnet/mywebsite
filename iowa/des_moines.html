Des Moines, Iowa

Des Moines has a way of changing first impressions. Many travelers arrive expecting a quiet Midwestern capital, only to discover a city where historic neighborhoods, gleaming downtown towers, vibrant parks, and lively local businesses come together with remarkable ease. Set along the Des Moines and Raccoon Rivers, the city has grown from a frontier settlement into Iowa’s economic and cultural center while preserving the friendliness that defines much of the Midwest. On any given morning, you’ll find runners following riverfront trails, professionals gathering in neighborhood cafés, and families exploring public parks that seamlessly connect the city’s urban core with its natural surroundings.

History is never far away here. Once an important military outpost and trading center, Des Moines evolved into a transportation hub before becoming one of the nation’s leading centers for insurance, finance, agriculture, and technology. Yet despite its growth, the city remains approachable. Brick-lined districts, restored warehouses, local boutiques, and welcoming public spaces encourage visitors to slow down and experience the city on foot. Every neighborhood reveals a different side of Des Moines, from the creative energy of the East Village to the historic charm of Sherman Hill and the lively atmosphere surrounding Gray’s Lake.

Spend a few days here, and you’ll quickly notice that Des Moines is defined less by famous landmarks than by the quality of everyday life. Seasonal festivals fill the calendar, farmers markets overflow with local produce, public art brightens downtown streets, and conversations with residents often turn into recommendations for hidden restaurants, neighborhood parks, or favorite coffee shops. It’s a city that rewards curiosity and offers a genuine sense of connection from the moment you arrive.
